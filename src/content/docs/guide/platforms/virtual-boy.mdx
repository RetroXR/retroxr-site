The Virtual Boy is the one machine in RetroXR that arguably works **better** here than it
did in 1995.

## The stereo actually works now

The original was a tabletop visor you pressed your face into. It genuinely rendered a
separate image per eye, which was real stereoscopy and not a trick, but you had to hold your head in
one exact position to see it, which is a large part of why nobody remembers it fondly.

RetroXR runs the core in **side-by-side mode**, so it outputs both eyes in one frame, then
feeds the left half to your left eye and the right half to your right. You get the depth
the hardware was aiming at, with **no sweet spot**. Lean in or move your head and it stays
correct.

<Aside type="tip" title="This is the same mechanism as the 3DS top screen">
	Both use per-eye sampling of a side-by-side frame. See
	[DS and 3DS](/guide/platforms/ds-and-3ds/).
</Aside>

## It is not a handheld

Despite the visor, RetroXR treats it as a console:

- **One controller port**: spawn a pad and plug it in.
- **A video-out cable that works**, so you can put it on a television as well.

## On a television, still in stereo

This is the detail worth knowing. A television does **not** get the raw side-by-side frame,
because that would read as two squashed copies side by side, which is useless.

Instead the TV is handed the **left half**, plus the offset that makes your right eye
sample the right half. So a connected television comes out **in stereo too**. The machine
is stereo end to end.

## On desktop

Without a headset there is no second eye to feed, so the eyepiece shows the left eye's
image flat. Everything else works normally.

## Red on black

The Virtual Boy's display was red LEDs on black, and no other colors existed. That is the
hardware, not a filter, and it is what you will see.

<Aside type="caution" title="Two shells, neither a fallback">
	There are two models: a detailed shell and the bipod stand-in authored in the scene
	itself. The shipping build uses the stand-in. Neither substitutes for the other, because they
	are separate models.
</Aside>

## The link cable

Nintendo designed a cable to join two Virtual Boys by their **EXT.** sockets and never sold
it, and no retail game shipped with a link mode. Since v0.10.0 the cable is here anyway:
spawn it from the Virtual Boy's card and plug one end into each unit. What it is for is a
handful of homebrew titles and Mario's Tennis with its two-player code patched back in.
It is a small library, but the ones that use it work. It needs Beetle VB in RetroXR's own build;
see [link cables](/guide/playing/link-cables/).

## Cores

Set the default in `CORES` → **Manager**, as described in [downloading and managing
cores](/guide/cores/downloading/). The side-by-side output mode is forced for you, so
there is nothing to configure to get the 3D working.

:::caution[Needs an in-headset pass]
The stereo path, the TV behavior and the port layout are read from the source and are
accurate. How comfortable it is to lean into the eyepiece has not been checked against a
running build.
:::
