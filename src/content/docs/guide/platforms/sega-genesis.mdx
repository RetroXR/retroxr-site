The Genesis, called the Mega Drive outside North America, takes cartridges from your
`roms/genesis` folder. It is also the console Sega bolted the most hardware onto, and
both of its add-ons are real objects here.

They stack in **opposite directions**, which is what makes the full tower possible:

| Unit | Where it goes | Its media |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **32X** | **Into** the cartridge slot, with its own slot on top | `roms/sega32x` |
| **Sega CD** | **Underneath**, with the console standing on it | `roms/segacd` |

See [expansion hardware](/guide/playing/expansion-hardware/) for how a stack goes together.

The console is a modeled **Genesis 2**, the smaller second model, and the
32X and both kinds of cartridge are modeled too, from photographs and caliper
measurements. Every trademark has been taken off them, which is a legal necessity rather
than a style. The Sega CD and the Sega CD Model 2 are still stand-ins.

Cartridges carry your scraped label art if the scraper found one. See [box
art](/guide/connecting/box-art/).

## The 32X

The 32X goes into the cartridge slot like a game, and has **a cartridge slot of its own on
top**. It is the only Sega expansion here that a game cartridge goes *into* rather than
past. Put a 32X cartridge in that upper slot.

A Genesis with a 32X on it is a different machine, and RetroXR switches to **picodrive**,
which is both halves at once. Take the 32X off and the console goes back to its usual core.

### Wiring a 32X

The picture goes through the 32X, as it did on the real hardware:

1. Run Sega's **Video Link Cable** from the Genesis's **A/V OUT** to the 32X's **A/V IN**.
2. Run the TV's lead from the 32X's **A/V OUT**.

A Model 1 Genesis has the older 8-pin A/V OUT, so it takes its own lead and the **32X
Conversion Cable**.

## The Sega CD

The Sega CD is a base the console **stands on**, and it loads from a **tray**: press the
unit's own eject button, put a disc in, close it.

On a Sega CD title the disc is the game and the console's cartridge slot stays empty. The
core is **genesis_plus_gx**.

<Aside type="caution" title="It needs a region BIOS">
	The Sega CD will not boot without the BIOS for its region, which you supply:

	| Region | File |
	| --- | --- |
	| Europe | `bios_CD_E.bin` |
	| USA | `bios_CD_U.bin` |
	| Japan | `bios_CD_J.bin` |

	They go in genesis_plus_gx's system folder, and the `BIOS / Extras` view in `CORES`
	shows which you have. Note the core declares all of them **optional**, which they are for a
	plain cartridge and are not for a disc.
</Aside>

### Where Sega CD games save

A Sega CD game saves where it did on the hardware: to the **backup RAM inside
the unit**, or to a **Backup RAM Cartridge** in the Genesis's slot if you have put one
there. The cartridge is offered on the Sega CD's card, and it is the way to carry saves
from one Sega CD to another, just as it was. Manage either from the console's **System
Settings** → **Saves** tab. A disc game's saves do not appear in the disc's own menu;
it shows a note pointing you here instead. See also [saves](/guide/connecting/saves/).

## Sonic & Knuckles

Spawned from the Genesis card: the **lock-on cartridge**. It goes
into the cartridge slot (or a 32X's slot) like any game, and has a slot of its own on top.
Put another Genesis cartridge in that slot to lock it on.

It needs two files in genesis_plus_gx's system folder: `sk.bin` **and** `sk2chip.bin`.

## The Power Base Converter

The adapter that goes into the cartridge slot and lets a Genesis play
**Master System** games. Put it in, put a Master System cartridge into it, and RetroXR
switches to genesis_plus_gx, which is both machines.

Master System hardware has one add-on of its own here too: the **FM Sound Unit**, which
attaches to a Master System and gets it the expanded soundtrack a number of Japanese
releases shipped with. Like the Power Base Converter it sets the core options for you.

## The Tower of Power

Both at once: the Sega CD on the bottom, the Genesis standing on it, the 32X in the
console's slot. Sega never sold it as one product and it was famously precarious in real
life, but here it simply stacks.

RetroXR treats the full tower as its own machine rather than as two add-ons that happen to
be present. It runs on **picodrive**, and **the disc is still what boots**. The tower
exists so that a Sega CD game can use the 32X's extra hardware, so the CD remains the thing
being played.

<Aside type="caution" title="Not yet verified against real games">
	Every Genesis combination here (32X alone, Sega CD alone, and the full tower) is
	written from each core's documented behavior and is marked **unverified** in RetroXR's
	own expansion catalog. The stacking, the media routing and the core switch are
	implemented; what has not been done is sitting down with real discs and cartridges and
	confirming each one boots and plays.

	If you try one, working or broken, that is genuinely useful to hear about on
	[Discord](https://discord.gg/pgUkRhafca) or in an
	[issue](https://github.com/RetroXR/RetroXR/issues).
</Aside>
