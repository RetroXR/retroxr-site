The Nintendo 64 takes cartridges from your `roms/n64` folder and plays them like any
other console here.

Underneath it goes the **[64DD](/guide/platforms/nintendo-64dd/)**, the magnetic disk drive
that only ever shipped in Japan and the reason the hatch in the console's floor is there.
The drive is taller than the console it carries, so the N64 stands on top of it, and a
cartridge can be in the console while a disk is in the drive.

## Cartridges

Since v0.8.0 an N64 cartridge is a modeled shell rather than a gray block, and RetroXR
dresses each one for the game in it:

- **The shape follows the region.** Japan's cartridges had their own rear notches and
  latches; North America and Europe shared a shape. Which one you get comes from the
  game's scraped region, or the ROM header if it has not been scraped.
- **The color follows the game.** The colored runs, such as gold Zelda and the Pokémon
  carts, are looked up by the ROM header, so a game that came in color comes out in
  color. Anything not in the table is gray.
- **The label is your box art scrape.** If the scraper found a label, it is printed onto
  the sticker. See [box art](/guide/connecting/box-art/).

To choose for yourself, **hold the game's row in the spawn menu for a second** instead of
tapping it. A sub-menu offers the body (Auto, USA/PAL or Japan) and the shell: Auto, any
of the known colors, or **Custom**, a color wheel with a metal-flake switch. The
[cartridges page](/guide/playing/cartridges-and-discs/) covers the same picker on the other
consoles.

## The controller, and the port in its back

New in **v0.6.0**: a modeled N64 controller, with the **expansion port** in its underside
that the console's peripherals plug into. Spawn a pak, push it into the back of the pad, and
the machine is told what is in there: RetroXR sets the running core's pak option for that
port to match.

Three paks go in it, and only one fits at a time, exactly as on the hardware.

| Pak | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Controller Pak** | 32 KiB of save storage, counted in pages |
| **Rumble Pak** | Force feedback in games that support it |
| **Transfer Pak** | Takes a Game Boy cartridge, for games that read one |

<Aside type="note" title="A pak works on a real gamepad too">
	The expansion port is not only on the virtual controller. If you play on a physical pad,
	the receiver it connects through has a port of its own. Otherwise the paks would exist
	and the one socket that took them would be on a controller you were not holding.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="The models are placeholders">
	The controller is modeled, but the three paks currently wear placeholder shapes. They seat,
	work and save regardless.
</Aside>

## Controller Pak saves

A Controller Pak is a **memory card that lives in a controller**, and RetroXR treats it as
one: point at a seated pak and open its panel and you get the notes written on it, counted
in **pages** the way every N64 game counts them. A pak has 123 usable pages, because the first
five hold the ID block, the index, its backup and the note table.

Each pak keeps its own file, named for the pak rather than for a game or a slot, so a pak
carries its notes between controllers and between machines. Its saves are stored per-pak as
a `.mpk` image.

<Aside type="caution" title="A pak whose file has gone missing runs unbacked">
	Only a pak this session created will have its image file made for it. If a pak comes
	back from a saved room and its file is gone, it deliberately runs **without backing**
	rather than silently starting a fresh blank one.

	That is on purpose: a blank pak where your notes used to be looks exactly like a wipe,
	and would become one the moment a game wrote to it.
</Aside>

Since v0.8.0 RetroXR also knows, from the N64 core's own game database, **which games save
to a Controller Pak**. When it restores a cartridge save from [RomM](/guide/connecting/romm/)
or a backup, it uses that to tell you whether the save is likely to be holding pak notes.
The database only ever says yes, so a game it does not list is unknown rather than
pak-free.

## Transfer Pak saves

The Transfer Pak takes a **Game Boy cartridge**, which is what Pokémon Stadium and friends
read. Socket a Game Boy cart into the pak and the console gets both halves: the cartridge's
ROM, and its save.

The important part is that **the save is the Game Boy cartridge's own**, kept exactly where
that cartridge's save is always kept. Play the cart in a Game Boy, carry it to the Transfer
Pak, and it is the same save. That is what the hardware did, and the whole point of the
accessory.

## The Expansion Pak

The N64's memory expansion goes in the console itself, under the lid on top, replacing the
Jumper Pak that ships there. Both are in RetroXR as of v0.6.0.

Fitting the Expansion Pak **sets the core option for you**, so a game that wants the extra
memory gets it and one that does not is unaffected. There is nothing to configure, because the
state of the console is the setting.

## The Voice Recognition Unit

New in **v0.8.0**: the VRU, the add-on that let you talk to *Hey You, Pikachu!* and drive
*Densha de GO! 64* by voice. It is a box that plugs into a **controller port**, socket 4,
which is where both VRU games expect it, with an ordinary pad in socket 1, and a
microphone on a cord that plugs into the box. It spawns with the microphone already in.

The VRU was never a plain microphone: the hardware recognized words itself and handed the
game the answer. RetroXR does the same with a speech recognizer, which needs a download:

<Steps>

1. Open `OPTIONS` → **Cores** → **BIOS / Extras** and pick the **Nintendo 64** page.

2. Install **Voice Recognition Unit speech: English**, **…: Japanese**, or both. The
   Japanese pack is for the Japanese releases; Densha de GO! 64 is played in English.

3. Turn on **Microphone** in `OPTIONS` if it is off, and pick your input there.

</Steps>

You do not press anything to talk. The game decides when it is listening, as it did with
the real unit. In Hey You, Pikachu! that is while you hold Z on your own controller. Your
voice is heard from where the microphone is, so keep it close: a microphone left on a
table across the room hears you faintly or not at all. Without the speech pack the VRU
still plugs in and the game still runs; it simply never understands you.

It runs on Mupen64Plus-Next, in RetroXR's own build. See also
[microphones](/guide/playing/controllers/).

<Aside type="note" title="The console wears a stand-in">
	The Nintendo 64 has a new primitive model as of v0.6.0, but not a modeled shell.
</Aside>
