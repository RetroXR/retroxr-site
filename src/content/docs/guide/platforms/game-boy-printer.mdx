The Game Boy Printer was a little thermal printer on the end of a link lead, and a
surprising number of games could use it: the [Game Boy Camera](/guide/platforms/game-boy-camera/)
above all, but also Pokémon's Pokédex, Link's Awakening DX's photo album, Super Mario Bros.
Deluxe's calendars and cards, and more.

As of **v0.11.0** it is in the room. The game sends the picture down the cable exactly as
it did to the hardware, the **paper feeds out of the slot line by line** as it prints, and
when you **tear it off** you are holding the print — and it is saved as an image file.

## Setting it up

<Steps>

1. **Download the printer's core.** Open `CORES` → **BIOS / Extras**, pick **Game Boy** (or
   Game Boy Color), and download the **Game Boy Printer** row. The printer runs a small
   emulator of its own, so it needs this once.

2. **Spawn the printer.** It is on the Game Boy's and the Game Boy Color's spawn cards, next
   to their link leads.

3. **Cable it to the Game Boy.** The printer's **EXT.IN** socket on the back is the *small*
   one, so:
   - from a **Game Boy Color**, use the **CGB-003** lead (small at both ends);
   - from an **original Game Boy**, fit the **DMG-14 adapter** into the Game Boy's large
     socket first, then run the CGB-003 from the adapter to the printer — which is how the
     printer was sold outside Japan.

4. **Switch the printer on** with its POWER switch, and the Game Boy too.

5. **Print from the game.** In the Game Boy Camera: open a photo, press **SELECT**, and
   choose print. Every game puts it somewhere different.

</Steps>

Either Game Boy core works on the other end of the lead — Gambatte or mGBA.

## The paper

The print comes out of the slot as it burns, and stays there, hanging out of the printer,
until you take it. Print several times and the strip just gets longer, the way a roll does.

- **FEED** advances blank paper.
- **To tear a print off**, take the paper with the **trigger**. (Not the grip, and not a
  brush of the hand: a print should never be thrown away by accident.) What you are holding
  is the print, as a picture at the paper's real size, and you can put it down anywhere in
  the room.
- Switching the printer **off** tears off whatever is hanging out first.

## Where prints are saved

Every torn print is written as a PNG:

| Platform | Folder |
| --- | --- |
| Windows | `%USERPROFILE%\retroxr\prints\` |
| Linux / macOS | `~/retroxr/prints/` |
| Quest | `prints/` in the [web file manager](/guide/adding-games/) |

The image is the printer's 160 dots across, centred on the real paper's width.

<Aside type="note" title="The sound is a best guess">
	You will hear the stepper motor and the print head. Nothing published describes what the
	real MGB-007 sounds like, and there was no unit to record, so the sound is synthesised
	from how the mechanism works. A recording of a real one would be very welcome — see
	[contributing](/contributing/).
</Aside>

## Over netplay

A printer cabled to a Game Boy joins that Game Boy in a netplay session, and every player
prints the same paper. To press FEED or tear the paper off, you need to be the player
holding it. Everyone needs to be on **v0.11.1 or later**, with updated cores — see
[netplay](/guide/connecting/netplay/).

## Known limits

- On **mGBA**, pulling the lead out **while a netplay session is running** can stall the
  session. Plugging in and printing are fine; on Gambatte, so is pulling it.
- Only a handful of printing games have been tried so far — the Game Boy Camera and Super
  Mario Bros. Deluxe are known to work. If another game will not print, please
  [report it](/guide/reference/reporting-bugs/).
