The Dreamcast plays discs from `roms/dreamcast/` on **Flycast**: `.chd`, `.cdi`, `.gdi` or `.cue`, or
an `.m3u` for a multi-disc game. As of **v0.9.0** it wears a modeled shell.

Its spawn card offers the console's own accessories: the **Visual Memory Unit**, the **Jump
Pack**, the **Microphone**, and Sega's **Stereo AV Cable**, which is the only lead that fits
the Dreamcast's AV OUT.

## The BIOS is optional

Flycast boots games without a Dreamcast BIOS. With one (`dc/dc_boot.bin` in the system
folder), you get the console's own startup swirl and its system menu. See
[BIOS files](/guide/cores/bios/).

## The VMU

The Dreamcast saved to a **Visual Memory Unit**: a memory card with a screen and buttons,
plugged into the top of the controller. RetroXR has had it since **v0.7.0**, and it is a
real object, the same as a [PlayStation memory card](/guide/platforms/playstation/).

<Steps>

1. Spawn a **Visual Memory Unit** from the Dreamcast's spawn card.

2. Push it into one of the **two expansion slots** on a Dreamcast pad. Only the front slot
   has a window for the screen, as on the real pad.

3. Power the console on and play. The game saves to that VMU.

</Steps>

**The VMU's screen works.** Whatever the game draws on it, whether Sonic Adventure's Chao, Power
Stone's character portraits or the health bars some games put there, shows on the unit in
your controller, and its beeps come from the unit too, rather than being burned into a
corner of the television.

<Aside type="caution" title="Plug it in before you switch on">
	The Dreamcast reads which VMUs are in which pads as the game starts. A VMU moved while the
	console is on is noticed at the next power-on, not straight away.
</Aside>

Each VMU is its own card, kept at:

```
libretro/save/memcards/vmu/<card name>.vmu
```

It is managed like any other card: rename it, see what is on it, delete saves, back it up
to RomM. See [game saves and backups](/guide/connecting/saves/).

### Playing a VMU on its own

The VMU was also a tiny handheld: some Dreamcast games downloaded a minigame onto it to play
away from the console. That works here. **Take the VMU out of the controller**, open its
menu, and choose a game to play on the unit itself, then press its **Play** button to start
it.

- Minigames a Dreamcast game put on the card are there to choose.
- Minigames you have as files (`.vms`, `.dci` or `.bin`) go in **`roms/vmu/`** and show up
  in the same menu, with their own icons.

A standalone VMU runs on the **VeMUlator** core; install it from `CORES` if the menu says
it is missing. Whatever progress you make is carried back onto the card when you stop.

As of **v0.9.0** VeMUlator also takes a **VMU BIOS**, either `en1005-19991026-315-6208-05.bin`
(US) or `jp1004-19980930-315-6208-01.bin` (Japan), and with one the unit shows its own
icons along the bottom of the screen. It is optional. Standalone VMUs also support save
states and rollback [netplay](/guide/connecting/netplay/). RetroXR's VeMUlator build can
link two VMUs, but there is no connector in the room to join them with yet.

## The Jump Pack

Sega's rumble pack goes into a pad's expansion slot exactly as a VMU does, and competes for
the same two slots. **No Jump Pack, no rumble**, because the console only sends vibration to a pad
that has one. A VMU in slot 1 and the Jump Pack in slot 2 is what most players had.

## The microphone

As of **v0.8.0** the **Dreamcast Microphone** works, fed by your headset's (or PC's)
microphone. It fits either expansion slot on the pad, and has no button of its own. Each
game talks on a pad button, A in Seaman and Y in Alien Front Online.

<Aside type="tip" title="Seaman is fussy">
	Seaman will not start unless the pad is in **port A**, with a **VMU in slot 1** and the
	**microphone in slot 2**.
</Aside>

See [controllers](/guide/playing/controllers/) for how microphones work across the
consoles that have one.

## Naomi and Atomiswave

Sega's arcade boards run on the same core, and as of **v0.9.0** each has a tile of its own
in the spawn menu (**Naomi**, **Naomi 2** and **Atomiswave**) rather than being lumped
in with the Dreamcast. Their BIOS files (`dc/naomi.zip`, `dc/naomi2.zip`, `dc/awbios.zip`
and a few per-game ones) are listed on the [BIOS](/guide/cores/bios/) page.
