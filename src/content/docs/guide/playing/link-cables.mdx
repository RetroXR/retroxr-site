Two machines in the same room can be **cabled together**, and the data really crosses. This
is not a netplay session dressed up as a wire: both machines are emulated here, in your
room, and the lead between them carries the same serial traffic the hardware did.

New in v0.4.0. **In v0.5.0 the link became event-driven** rather than polled: the cores
wake each other when there is something to send instead of checking a shared lock on every
step, which cuts the contention three to fourfold and is felt as a smoother run with up to
**four cores** cabled together.

## What can be linked

| Cable | Machines | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Link Cable** (Game Boy) | Two original Game Boys | Nothing extra |
| **Link Cable** (Game Boy Color) | Two Game Boy Colors, or one and a [Game Boy Printer](/guide/platforms/game-boy-printer/) | Nothing extra |
| **Link Cable Adapter** | A Color's small plug into an original Game Boy's large socket | — |
| **GBA link cable** | Up to four Game Boy Advances | A GBA BIOS for single-pak play |
| **GC-GBA Cable** | A GameCube and up to four GBAs | A GBA BIOS |
| **PlayStation Link Cable** | Two PlayStations | Nothing extra |
| **i.LINK Cable** and **i.LINK Hub** | Up to six PlayStation 2s | A PS2 BIOS, as always |
| **Ethernet Cable** and **Ethernet Hub** | Up to eight GameCubes and PlayStation 2s on one LAN | A network adapter in each console |
| **Link Cable** (Saturn) | Two Sega Saturns | A Saturn BIOS, as always |
| **Gear-to-Gear Cable** | Two Game Gears | Nothing extra |
| **ComLynx Cable** | Up to eight Atari Lynxes | A Lynx BIOS |
| **Link Cable** (Neo Geo Pocket) | Two Neo Geo Pockets, either model | Nothing extra |
| **Communication Cable** | Two WonderSwans, either model | Nothing extra |
| **JagLink Cable** | Two Atari Jaguars | Nothing extra |
| **Link Cable** (Virtual Boy) | Two Virtual Boys | Nothing extra |
| *No cable: infrared* | Two Game Boy Colors facing each other | Nothing extra |

Most of these arrived in v0.9.0 and v0.10.0, the Ethernet LAN and the printer in v0.11.0.
Several of these machines will not start at all without their BIOS, cable or no cable,
so see [BIOS files](/guide/cores/bios/).

Each lead is offered on the **system's own card** in `SPAWN` → **Systems**. Open the card
for the machine you are cabling and the right cable is listed with its controllers. Push
each end into the matching socket:
the link port on a handheld's edge, the SERIAL I/O socket on the back of a PlayStation, or,
for the GBA-to-GameCube cable, a **controller port** on the console.

Every machine has its own lead rather than one cable for all. A Game Boy lead is not a GBA
lead, and neither is a PlayStation's, exactly as on the shelf. Even the Game Boys have two:
the original's lead is large at both ends, the Color's is small at both and also fits the
Advance and the printer. The **Link Cable Adapter** takes a small plug in its back and goes
into an original Game Boy's large socket, which is how a Color reaches an original.

The GBA lead carries a junction in the middle, which is how a third and fourth handheld
chain on; most of the others are a plain two-machine cord. The GC-GBA cable is listed on
**both** the GameCube's card and the Game Boy Advance's, so you can find it from either
end.

## Multiplayer on one cartridge

The GBA cable does **single-pak** as well as multi-pak, which is the mode where one
cartridge feeds up to four handhelds:

<Steps>

1. Spawn as many Game Boy Advances as you want players, and enough link cables to chain
   them.

2. Put the game in **one** of them. The others stay empty.

3. **Plug the purple end into the machine holding the cartridge.** That is the host, and
   single-pak will not start if the cable is the other way round.

4. Power up. The host uploads the game to the others over the wire, as the hardware did.

</Steps>

Single-pak boots the empty handhelds from their BIOS, so **a GBA BIOS is required** for it.
See [BIOS files](/guide/cores/bios/).

## The GameCube cable

The GameCube end of that lead goes into a **controller port**, not a link socket, because
a GameCube had no link port. Four cables means four handhelds on one console.

Two things worth knowing before you judge it:

- **Games that use the GBA as a second screen work best with an empty handheld.** Those
  titles upload their own program over the cable, so a GBA holding its own cartridge is a
  legitimate way to plug it in but not the pairing the game was written for.
- The lead costs a few percent of the frame rate, not a slice of it. Expect one hitch
  around a second long while the program uploads, then nothing.

## More than two: hubs and chains

Three kinds of lead join more than a pair.

- **i.LINK.** A PlayStation 2's i.LINK socket is on its back panel. One cable joins two
  consoles; for more, run each console's cable into the **i.LINK Hub**, which has six
  sockets. Gran Turismo 3's i.LINK Battle takes all six, and Time Crisis II links two.
- **Ethernet.** The PlayStation 2's **Network Adapter** and the GameCube's **Broadband
  Adapter** are expansion cards, and the network socket is on the card: seat it in the
  console and the socket becomes the console's. Seating it is also what switches the
  console's network hardware on, so put the card in **before** you power up. A cable
  between two consoles is a LAN of two; the **Ethernet Hub** takes eight. Mario Kart:
  Double Dash!! shows LAN MODE, and Gran Turismo 4's Multi-LAN Race takes up to six.
  Each console is given an address of its own, so a game's "obtain automatically"
  setting just works. A GameCube and a PS2 can share one hub, and they simply ignore each other.
- **ComLynx.** Every ComLynx plug has a socket on its back, so a third Lynx plugs in where
  the last one did, up to eight on one wire as on the real cable.

## Infrared

A Game Boy Color has an infrared window, and since v0.10.0 two of them can talk through it
for Mystery Gift and the games that swap items by beaming them. There is no cable: **point
the two windows at each other**.

They pair while both are switched on, within about 60 cm of each other, roughly facing,
and with nothing solid in between. The window turns red while it is paired. Hands, and
anything being held, do not block it. A Game Boy Color with a link cable to another machine
is left out: it listens to the cable or to the window, not both.

## The Game Boy Printer

The printer is the far end of a Game Boy link lead, and has a page of its own:
[Game Boy Printer](/guide/platforms/game-boy-printer/).

## Mixing cores

A link is between **machines**, not between copies of one core. A Game Boy running
gambatte and a Game Boy running mGBA join the same cable and trade bytes quite happily.

## This wants a PC

<Aside type="caution" title="Recommended on PCVR">
	A link is two or more cores running at once in the same room, and that is more than a
	standalone headset comfortably carries. It runs on a Quest, and you should try it, but PCVR
	is the place to expect it to be smooth. Four Game Boy Advances with three of them empty is
	known to stutter at first while the program upload happens.
</Aside>

## Where the support comes from

Serial link is not something libretro offered. RetroXR added a core-agnostic serial link
API [upstream to
RetroArch](https://github.com/libretro/RetroArch/pull/19454), and the cores it runs are
forks carrying the other half:
[mGBA](https://github.com/RetroXR/mgba),
[gambatte](https://github.com/RetroXR/gambatte-libretro),
[Dolphin](https://github.com/RetroXR/dolphin),
[pcsx_rearmed](https://github.com/RetroXR/pcsx_rearmed),
[PCSX2](https://github.com/RetroXR/ps2),
[Beetle Saturn](https://github.com/RetroXR/beetle-saturn-libretro),
[Genesis Plus GX](https://github.com/RetroXR/Genesis-Plus-GX),
[Beetle Lynx](https://github.com/RetroXR/beetle-lynx-libretro),
[Beetle NeoPop](https://github.com/RetroXR/beetle-ngp-libretro),
[Beetle WonderSwan](https://github.com/RetroXR/beetle-wswan-libretro),
[Beetle VB](https://github.com/RetroXR/beetle-vb-libretro) and
[Virtual Jaguar](https://github.com/RetroXR/virtualjaguar-libretro), plus the
[printer](https://github.com/RetroXR/gbprinter-libretro), a core of RetroXR's own. The
core manager downloads them for you; there is nothing to build.

A cable to a core that is **not** one of these joins nothing, because the stock build has
no far end for the wire. If a link seems dead, check in the core manager that the machine
is running the RetroXR build. Both ends also have to speak the same protocol, and v0.11.1
renamed them, so linking across [netplay](/guide/connecting/netplay/) needs every player
on v0.11.1 or later with updated cores.
